menu_bookIntroduction

Wolfgang Iser argued that central to the reading of every literary work is the interaction between its structure and its recipient. But he also believed a text cannot adapt itself to each reader it comes in contact with. Digital poetry, which can respond to clicks, scrolling, or reader input, puts that limit to the test.

visibilityHyperreading

N. Katherine Hayles calls screen-based reading hyperreading, and argues it works differently than reading print:

Recent studies indicate that hyperreading not only requires different reading strategies than close reading but also may be involved with changes in brain architecture that makes close reading more difficult to achieve.

N. Katherine Hayles, “How We Read: Close, Hyper, Machine,” 2010
